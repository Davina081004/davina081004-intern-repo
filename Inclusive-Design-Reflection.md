## Research & Learn

**Vulnerable populations and digital challenges**
- This includes people with disabilities, neurodivergent people (ADHD, autism), children, elderlies, and people dealing with mental health struggles.
- In digital spaces they can face cluttered screens, too many notifications, confusing navigation, and unclear instructions.

**Ethical considerations for neurodivergent users**
- Avoid overwhelming designs (too many colours, pop-ups and alerts).
- Respect sensory needs, for example by letting users turn off sounds and animations.

**Making things more accessible for ADHD and autism**
- Use simple, short language and keep navigation predictable and consistent.
- Reduce cognitive load by showing one step at a time.

**Ways to support neurodivergent teammates**
- Be clear and specific in messages.
- Respect different working styles and flexibility.

## Task

**First person account**
I read ‘6 Productivity Tools That Actually Work for AuDHD Brains’ by Nicola Knobel. She has ADHD and autism, and she was diagnosed late. She burned out from forcing herself into neurotypical productivity systems. What works for her is flexible: visual timers, body doubling, soft timeblocking with small rewards, task stacking, permission slips like ‘You are allowed to rest’, and a simple checklist for burnout days. What stood out to me is that strict systems made her feel guilty, and she needed ones that are kind and can bend.

**One improvement for Focus Bear**
Focus Bear could show a bear that grows the more you focus, like how the Forest app grows trees. Seeing progress makes focus feel rewarding, and it gives users with ADHD a small dopamine boost for staying on task. To keep it supportive, the bear should never shrink or disappear if the user stops early or misses a day, so users do not feel guilty about it.

**Supportive response to a struggling user**
“Hey there, don't worry,struggling with focus is really common and you’re not doing anything wrong. Let’s start small: pick just one task and set a 10 minute timer. If that feels like too much, we can make it even smaller. You don’t need to be perfect, and every little step counts.”
