## Research & Learn

**Risk of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard**
- Users often hunch and look down because the laptop and keyboard are joined together. When the laptop is placed on a table, this can cause neck strain and shoulder pain.
- If the laptop is placed higher, the wrists and hands have to bend upwards at an uncomfortable angle.
- Laptop screens are smaller than monitors, so they can cause eye strain and headaches.

**Ergonomic equipment that can improve posture when working on a laptop**
- Laptop stands adjust the height of the laptop, which lifts the screen to eye level and prevents hunching and neck strain.
- External monitors have larger screens, so we can sit further back to lessen eye strain.
- Ergonomic chairs support our back and can be adjusted to a comfortable height, so our feet stay flat on the floor.

**Adjustments that should be made to monitor height, chair position, and desk setup for a healthier workspace**
- Monitor height: the top of the screen should be at eye level or slightly below, to prevent hunching and neck strain.
- Chair position: adjust the chair so our feet are flat on the floor, and our back is supported by the chair.
- Desk: it should not be cluttered or messy, and it should be around elbow height so our wrists can rest comfortably and stay straight.

**Daily habits that reduce the impact of prolonged laptop use**
- Take screen breaks regularly, such as the 20-20-20 rule (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds), or use eye drops to lessen eye strain.
- Take light movement breaks (stretch or walk) every hour to avoid sitting all day.
- Do quick posture checks often to make sure our shoulders are not hunched, our back is supported and the screen is at eye level.
