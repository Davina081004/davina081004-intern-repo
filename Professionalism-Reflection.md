## Research & Learn

**Professional vs unprofessional behaviour**
- Professional: coming to meetings prepared, giving daily updates, replying on time, and using clear subjects on calendar invites.
- Unprofessional: waiting around for instructions, winging meetings, ghosting your project, ignoring feedback, and multitasking during meetings.

**Respectful communication in remote teams**
- Use a polite tone and keep messages clear and short.
- Assume good intent and talk about the idea, not the person, even when you disagree.
- Reply on time.

**Giving and receiving feedback professionally** 
- Giving: be specific, focus on actions not people, and be kind with words.
- Receiving: listen attentively, ask for clarification instead of getting defensive.

## Reflection

**Great teamwork I have witnessed or experienced** 
As Design Director for the Deakin Malaysian Association, our team worked well because we shared clear timelines and kept communicating even when we faced blockers.

**Steps to make my communication clear, respectful and inclusive**
Use simple words and keep a polite tone, double check before sending, and ask for others’ opinions.

**Ways to create a positive, fun and professional environment**
Be friendly and supportive, respect everyone’s ideas while still doing my work on time.

**Ways to prepare for meetings**
Read the brief, check my progress, bring questions and an agenda, and take notes.

**How to be proactive**
Suggest next steps instead of waiting, give daily updates, and try to solve things myself before asking.

**Ways to follow up with colleagues**
- Start with email (they reply within 48 hours), and send a polite nudge if there is no reply.
- Use Discord or Teams if I need a reply within 24 hours.
- Use SMS if it is urgent (2 hours), and only call for a true emergency.
- Escalate when the deadline is close or I have had no reply in that time.

**Things I will not do**
Wait around for instructions, come to meetings unprepared, ghost my project, ask questions I could Google, ignore feedback, multitask in meetings, or send vague invite subjects.
